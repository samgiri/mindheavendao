# MindHeavenDAO Foundation Documentation Standards

**Document ID:** MHF-002  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft for founder review

## Purpose

These standards keep MindHeavenDAO records understandable, traceable, and honest about their authority. They apply to governance, policy, economic, technical, security, legal, research, and operating documents.

## Required document header

Every controlled document should identify:

- title and document ID;
- version;
- status;
- owner or maintainer;
- approval authority;
- approval date, when approved;
- last updated date;
- related decisions or superseded documents; and
- classification where necessary.

## Status vocabulary

Use only the following statuses:

- **Working draft** — actively being written and not ready for decision.
- **Draft for review** — complete enough for structured comments.
- **Proposed for approval** — review completed and a formal decision requested.
- **Approved** — accepted by the stated authority.
- **Ratified** — accepted through the applicable constitutional governance process.
- **Superseded** — replaced by a newer identified document.
- **Archived** — retained for history but no longer operational.

Draft material must never be described as approved, final, guaranteed, launched, or live.

## Document families

- `MHF` — Foundation charter, manifesto, standards, and institutional policy.
- `MHC` — Constitution and governance policy.
- `MHT` — Technical architecture and protocol specifications.
- `MHE` — Economic and token specifications.
- `MHS` — Security, threat models, audits, and incident procedures.
- `MHL` — Legal, compliance, and jurisdictional analysis.
- `MHR` — Research and evidence reviews.
- `MHO` — Operational procedures and implementation plans.

## Writing rules

Official documents must:

- use clear language before specialist terminology;
- distinguish confirmed decisions from proposals and examples;
- state assumptions and unresolved decisions;
- avoid guaranteed-return, investment, medical, or legal conclusions;
- define material terms once and use them consistently;
- identify the accountable owner of actions;
- include measurable criteria where practical;
- link to source evidence and decision records;
- disclose meaningful conflicts and limitations; and
- avoid silently changing approved values.

## Change control

Material changes require:

1. a new version;
2. a concise change summary;
3. a linked decision record;
4. review by the relevant subject owner;
5. approval under the applicable authority; and
6. preservation of the superseded version.

Spelling, formatting, broken links, and non-substantive clarity edits may be treated as patches but must not change meaning.

## Decision hygiene

Every consequential decision should record:

- the question decided;
- available options;
- the selected option;
- rationale;
- decision-maker or vote;
- date;
- dependencies;
- risks and dissent;
- effective date; and
- documents or code affected.

Silence is not approval. An unanswered question belongs in the open-decisions register.

## Economic and technical safeguards

Token, reward, referral, treasury, governance, identity, health, and AI specifications must clearly separate:

- planning references from executable rules;
- example data from live data;
- off-chain controls from on-chain enforcement;
- gross rates from actual funded liabilities;
- token utility from financial expectations;
- governance intent from deployed permissions; and
- wellness support from medical diagnosis or treatment.

No mainnet deployment or fundraising is authorized merely because a document is approved.

## Repository practice

Foundation knowledge and engineering implementation should remain logically separated. Changes should be made through scoped branches and pull requests, with validation appropriate to the content.

An approved homepage or brand asset must not be redesigned by documentation work. SatoshiLab.ai should be identified consistently as MindHeavenDAO’s technology advisory company unless an approved decision changes that role.

## Review checklist

Before approval, confirm:

- the document has a clear authority and status;
- terminology matches related documents;
- confirmed and open decisions are separated;
- figures and formulas are internally consistent;
- risks and limitations are visible;
- legal, health, privacy, and financial claims are appropriately bounded;
- implementation does not exceed the approved policy; and
- the decision log and index are updated.
